# J10 — the free/refill side of growmix against its gap: Ir ceilings and the kernel share (`bench`: measurement only; nothing changes)

Generated by `node scripts/j10_report.mjs` from `docs/perf/j10/raw` (`docs/perf/j10/j10.sh`, main `fefcf970`) and the J7 split of the same scenario. Do not edit by hand.

## 1. Question

J7 named `flush_class_internal`, `lane_miss` and `try_carve_batch` as the Sefer-side functions of the growmix gap. Which part of that cost is the segment-table lookup,
what would the next Ir lever (a hash-probe exit in the own-free lane, the way WILD-2 added the `own_cache` exit) save at most, and how much of the growmix time gap can any Ir lever reach?

## 2. Method

- Judge `g1_wild_judge growmix sefer 1 0.25` under callgrind with line tables (`--dump-line=yes`), a build of one git archive of the measured commit (`docs/perf/j10/raw/ident.txt`), alignment RUSTFLAGS as in J6.
  Whole process 233691421 Ir over 731010 operations (the judge's own `ops`) = 319.7 Ir/op, including the judge's bookkeeping, which both arms carry.
- Self Ir per function with inlined callees credited to their own source file (`docs/perf/j10/lines.mjs`, `files.mjs`); the segment-table part of a function is its self Ir in `segment_table/hash.rs` and `segment_table/segment_table_impl.rs`.
  Call counts are the `calls=` records of the callgrind file.
- Time split of the growmix gap from J7 (Linux, median of 36 runs per arm): sefer minus mimalloc = 40.6 ns/op, user 40 ms, kernel 85 ms, 37349 page faults. Ir gap per op (J7 build, `docs/perf/j7/raw`): 325.8 − 249.9 = 75.9.
- The last column of section 4 reads the user-time gap as proportional to the Ir gap (40.0 ms over 75.9 Ir/op). That is an assumption, not a measurement; the Ir columns do not depend on it.

## 4. Ceilings

| lever | Ir/op | share of the Ir gap (75.9 Ir/op) | share of the CPU gap (user + kernel = 125 ms) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| segment-table lookup cost, lane_miss + flush_class_internal (whole, cannot be removed) | 27.6 | 36.4 % | 11.7 % |
| hash-probe exit in the lane (model: lane_miss minus its table part, plus the second chance per call) | 13.3 | 17.6 % | 5.6 % |

The model of the hash-probe exit: a free that misses the memo and the `own_cache` pays the lookup (35.0 Ir/call in `lane_miss`) and the lane's own acceptance steps (18.7 Ir/call, the measured self cost of `lane_second_chance`) instead of the whole chain (87.4 Ir/call);
saving 33.7 Ir/call over 289112 calls. It is a ceiling by construction: it assumes the new exit costs no more than those two measured parts.

## 5. Reading

- The kernel part is 68.0 % of the growmix CPU gap (85 of 125 ms; 37349 more page faults than mimalloc, the memory Sefer returns and re-faults: Retain, card 146). No Ir lever touches it.
- Removing the **entire** segment-table lookup cost on the free side (impossible: ownership is validated by it) would be 27.6 Ir/op, 36.4 % of the Ir gap and about 11.7 % of the CPU gap.
- The realistic lever, the hash-probe exit, is at most 13.3 Ir/op: 4.1 % of Sefer's 325.8 Ir/op, 17.6 % of the Ir gap, about 5.6 % of the CPU gap. Under the repository's rule (time claim only with a disjoint paired interval) it cannot be read in time on a 40.6 ns/op gap.
- `grow64`, `grow1k`, `regrow` have hash probes under 0.1 % of small frees (`SEGMODEL_OWN_CACHE.md`): the lever does not exist there.

## 6. Verdict

No prototype. The free/refill Ir levers of growmix add up to a few percent of the Ir gap and a smaller share of the time gap, which is mostly kernel time. The step that moves growmix, grow64's page-fault half, grow1k and regrow is the memory-return policy (Retain, owner decision, card 146); the hash-probe exit stays a recorded candidate of at most 13.3 Ir/op.
This report changes nothing.
