# RP-2 — the owner's cost of reclaiming a wave of foreign frees (`bench`, measurement only, no runtime change)

**Class:** `bench` — a measurement and attribution report; a loss against mimalloc found by the RP-1 probe. No
allocator source changed in the repository; the counters and experiments below ran on scratch copies of `main` @
`c0ee7dfb` (the scratch patches are kept as text next to the report). Tables and the summary CSV are generated by
`scripts/rp2_report.mjs` from `docs/perf/rp2/`; the prose cites them. Platform: Linux (WSL2) only.

## 0. Summary

1. **The shape.** The owner thread allocates S segments' worth of 64 B (or 1024 B) blocks; three other threads free
   all of them (a wave of foreign frees); the threads are joined; the owner allocates the same number of blocks again,
   timing every call. This is a producer / consumer hand-off of a whole batch.
2. **A loss of the owner's second phase, a win in the wave.** With 16 segments x 64 B the owner phase takes
   {{OWNER_RATIO}} against mimalloc, the longest single `alloc` call {{MAX_PAUSE}}. The wave of foreign frees itself is
   faster for sefer than for mimalloc (section A), so the whole shape (wave + owner phase) is a smaller but real loss
   (sum of medians, sefer / mi: 16 x 64 B {{WO_16_64}}, 16 x 1024 B {{WO_16_1024}}, section A). p99.9 of sefer is below mimalloc's for the 64 B shapes: the loss is
   a total cost plus rare long calls, not a general slowness of the hot path.
3. **The mechanism, by counters.** One discovery call (`process_ingress`) takes **every** notified slot and drains
   each segment completely: {{INGRESS}}. Segments emptied by the drain are pooled up to the pool cap of 4 and
   released to the OS beyond it: {{DRAIN_OUT}}. After that only the pool and the current segment hold reusable
   blocks; {{CARVE_PCT}} of the owner's blocks then come from bump-carve of {{RESERVE}} freshly reserved segments, and
   every one of those refills first runs a discovery call that finds nothing. The longest call is the one that drains
   all notified segments; it grows with S ({{PAUSE_SEG}}).
4. **The instruction cost.** callgrind of the owner phase: {{IR_BLOCK}}. By function (section C2): the drain of 16
   segments costs {{DRAIN_IRB}} Ir per block, a failing discovery {{DISC_IR}} Ir per call without its drains, bump-carve
   {{CARVE_IRB}} Ir per carved block. Time follows instructions (section A against section C).
5. **What was tried (scratch, section D).** A larger pool (`Profile` Throughput, 8 segments) does not help: more blocks
   come from freelists (carve share {{POOL8_CARVE}}) but the owner phase is slower ({{POOL8_SUM}}). Not releasing a segment that a
   drain emptied (E1) helps: owner phase 16 x 64 B {{E1_16_64}}, 16 x 1024 B {{E1_16_1024}} (variant / base, ms); the 4 x 64 B longest
   call is unchanged ({{E1_4_MAX}}). Skipping discovery for 63 refills after a miss (E2, an upper bound, not safe) changes the owner phase
   instructions by {{E2_IR}} and its time by {{E2_WALL}} (variant / base), and the instructions of the growth
   scenarios `grow64` / `grow1k` by {{E2_GROW}}.
6. **What it does not say.** It does not say that the release policy is wrong: pool and release are the RSS policy
   (open item RET-1 / `Retain`). It does not say how the loss looks on Windows (not measured), and it does not
   measure RSS.

## 1. What was measured

- **Identity.** `main` @ `c0ee7dfb` (tree `f67aa3d5...`), `git archive`; probe `g1_rp1_probe.rs.txt` (sha256
  `c16324cf3839c0a493859d6f952d78899e5f57d4c6ba3cea4c23ddb1a2bf9ab4`; its `sefer8` arm and the `RP1_NOSTATS` switch were
  added after the latency sweep of section A, the measured code path is unchanged). The counter build is the same tree
  plus `patch_counters.py`; experiment E1 patches one condition in `finish_small_drain` (`rp1e1.sh`), E2 adds a skip
  counter in `refill_class_bump_impl` (`rp1e2.sh`). rustc 1.98.1, Linux 6.18 (WSL2), i7-11800H, shared with other
  sessions.
- **Entry point.** The probe drives `SeferAlloc` (the layer the `#[global_allocator]` uses) and `MiMalloc` through
  `#[inline(never)]` shims over `GlobalAlloc`; one arm per process; std keeps using the system allocator. The owner is
  the main thread; three scoped threads free (foreign frees); every owner call is timed with `rdtsc`, converted to ns by
  the loop's own wall time.
- **Statistics.** Section A: medians of 7 processes per arm, arms interleaved. Sections B, C: deterministic counters
  and callgrind (the harness statistics are switched off with `RP1_NOSTATS` so that the sort of the latency array does
  not enter the instruction counts). Section D: medians of 7 interleaved processes.
- **Config evidence for the pool-8 arm.** The counter build run with the Throughput profile reports 8 pooled and 6
  released segments (section B, row "pool 8"): the larger pool did take effect.
