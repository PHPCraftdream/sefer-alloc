# MT1 at T=1 — where the larson / mstress gap to mimalloc sits (`bench`, measurement only, no runtime change)

**Class:** `bench` — a measurement and attribution report. No allocator source changed; no `production`
composition changed. Tables and the summary CSV are generated by `scripts/mt1_t1_report.mjs` from
`docs/perf/mt1c/`; the prose cites them. Platform: Linux (WSL2) only; the Windows MT numbers of LM-1
(`docs/perf/LM1_LOSS_MAP.md`: larson T=1 1.098, mstress T=1 1.239, medians of 6) are not re-measured here.

## 0. Summary

1. **Instructions (callgrind, 300 000 steps, T=1).** mstress: sefer 70 417 758 against mimalloc 63 674 269
   (70 417 758 / 63 674 269 = 1.106); larson: 68 719 216 against 64 158 764 (1.071). WILD-2 (PR #158) changes
   them by -130 and +142 Ir, i.e. it does not touch this gap.
2. **The gap is in `free`, not in `alloc`.** Inclusive Ir per call, shim frame included (418 135 allocs and as many
   frees in mstress): sefer `alloc` 38.3 against mimalloc 53.9 (sefer is cheaper), sefer `free`
   53.1 against mimalloc 24.8 (sefer is dearer). Net per alloc+free pair: sefer
   91.4 against 78.7; times 418 135 pairs that is 5311217 Ir (5311217 / 418135 = 12.7 per pair) of the whole-run gap 6 743 489
   (5311217 / 6743489 = 0.788); the remainder lies outside the two entry-point frames and is not attributed further.
3. **The cold magazine events are not the gap.** mstress runs 20 757 parks and 20 788 refills against 20 038
   `_mi_malloc_generic` calls of mimalloc; their inclusive Ir is 3 377 405 against 2 813 303, a difference of
   564 102 Ir, 564 102 / 6 743 489 = 0.084 of the gap. An earlier reading of `perf record` cycle shares (cold
   events about 60 % of the cycle gap) is withdrawn: sampling shares carry skid and the call counts do not support it.
4. **mstress is an instruction-count gap with no stall component.** Cycles sefer / mi 1.078 against instructions
   1.108, IPC 2.879 against 2.802. Branch misses (2.397 M against 2.362 M), L1I misses, dTLB misses and L1D
   misses (1.027) do not differ. **larson** has a second component: cycles 1.166 against instructions 1.078, IPC
   3.085 against 3.337, with fewer branch and L1D misses for sefer; about 0.28 of the cycle gap sits in the std
   `mpmc` receiver (the harness' channel, whose nodes come from the allocator under test); the cause is not found.
5. **What this says about a lever.** The mstress lever is the length of the `free` path (the accept path alone is
   49 instructions in the R123 disassembly, `docs/perf/G1_R123_FREE_LANE.md`; the 53.1 here also contains the
   shim frame and the cold events). AS-1, R123 and A0' (NO-GO) already worked that path; a further cut has to keep every
   safety check and must pass the magnitude rule. Nothing in this report is a candidate by itself.

## 1. What was measured

- **Identity.** Base `c0ee7dfb` (`main` after PR #157, tree `f67aa3d5...`), built from a `git archive` of that
  commit; "WILD-2" is the head of branch `g1-wild2` (PR #158). The probe is `examples/g1_mt_one.rs` of branch `g1-mt1`
  (commit `f969d664`), kept verbatim as `g1_mt_one.rs.txt` next to this report (sha256
  `67aa6fea52bdaa97e08c6baa4b94d2cc29ec9b09851bd36fe7beda4c4c186c22`) and registered as an example by `mt1c.sh`.
  Binary hashes: `ident.txt`. rustc 1.98.1, Linux 6.18 (WSL2), i7-11800H, shared with other sessions.
- **Entry point.** The probe drives `malloc-bench-rs` through `#[inline(never)]` shims that call
  `GlobalAlloc::alloc` / `dealloc` of `SeferAlloc` (the layer the `#[global_allocator]` uses) and of `MiMalloc`;
  std itself keeps using the system allocator. One arm per process, one thread (T=1), 300 000 steps under
  callgrind, 3 000 000 steps under `perf stat` / `perf record` (user mode, `cycles:u`).
- **Statistics.** callgrind numbers are deterministic. `perf stat` rows are medians of interleaved runs (7 for
  cycles, instructions, L1D misses; 5 for branches, L1I, dTLB, loads); the min-to-max cycle range of sefer is in the
  table. The arithmetic printed in the tables is asserted by the script.

## 3. Reading

- **mstress T=1.** The deterministic gap is 6 743 489 Ir over 300 000 steps. The measured alloc/free pairs explain
  the share printed in section 0 item 2; the cold magazine events explain 0.084 of it. `perf stat` shows the same
  gap in cycles at a slightly better IPC, so on Linux this is a path-length gap, not a stall. The `free` side costs
  more than twice what mimalloc's `mi_free` costs per call, the `alloc` side costs less; a change that lengthens
  `alloc` to shorten `free` is not visible in this data.
- **larson T=1.** The instruction gap (1.071) is smaller than the cycle gap (1.166). Branch misses, L1I, dTLB and
  L1D misses are not higher for sefer, so the extra cycles are neither of those. About 0.28 of the cycle gap is
  attributed (by `perf record` share times `perf stat` cycles, a hint with skid) to the std `mpmc` receiver, the
  harness' channel, whose nodes are allocated by the allocator under test; cycles of that function differ between
  arms although its instruction count is identical in both (`callgrind`: 7 500 049 + 5 700 039 Ir in both).
  That is a candidate for placement or dependency effects of the memory the allocator returns, not a measured cause.
- **WILD-2.** Not relevant to T=1 mstress / larson (-130 and +142 Ir).
- **Windows.** The LM-1 ratios (1.098, 1.239) are larger than the Linux ones (1.071 / 1.106 in Ir, 1.166 / 1.078
  in cycles); this report does not explain the difference and does not measure Windows.

## 4. Limits

- One machine, shared with other sessions; Linux (WSL2) only. callgrind rows are deterministic; `perf stat` rows
  are medians of 5-7 interleaved runs and the larson cycles of sefer span 211.4-228.8 M.
- `perf record` shares are sampling-based (skid); they are used only as an attribution hint in section 3.
- The call-tree extract attributes inlined callees by file and line; the mimalloc `_mi_page_retire` row is the
  inclusive Ir as printed and its call count is not used.
- The "49 instructions" of the R123 accept path is a static count of the hit path; the per-call Ir of this report
  is a dynamic inclusive count that includes the shim frame and the cold events. They are different quantities.

## 5. What is left

- Larson: find the cause of the cycles that are not instructions (placement / dependency of the memory returned to
  the channel nodes); a first test is `perf record` with annotation of the receiver function for both arms.
- Mstress: any further cut of the `free` path must keep every safety check and pass `scripts/g1_magnitude_verdict.mjs`.

## 6. Reproduction

- `mt1c.sh` (builds base and WILD-2 from `git archive` tars, registers `g1_mt_one.rs.txt` as an example, runs
  callgrind), `mt1p.sh` (cycles / instructions / L1D), `mt1b.sh` (branches, L1I), `mt1t.sh` (dTLB, loads),
  `mt1r2.sh` (`perf record` shares), `mt1calls.sh` and `mt1fc.sh` (call-tree extracts). They are kept as run, with
  the scratch directory replaced by `<scratch dir>`; they need WSL2 with valgrind and a `perf` binary matching the
  kernel.
- Tables and CSV: `node scripts/mt1_t1_report.mjs` (writes `docs/perf/mt1c/tables.md`,
  `docs/perf/MT1_T1_DECOMPOSITION_summary.csv` and this file from `doc_head.md`, `doc_tail.md` and the tables).
