# R20 PRF-04 heap reuse (ReuseFirst hint spill) — official v2 gate, verdict GO (deterministic; native deferred)

Runtime change (`perf(runtime)`): candidate ReuseFirst becomes the default registry claim path. When a thread
releases its heap, the hint it displaces is spilled into a FREE-slot bitmap, so a later claim reuses a FREE heap
instead of materialising a new one. No feature flag. The native latency and RSS axes stay DEFERRED by the owner
decision recorded in the preregistration.

## 2. History: run01 and protocol v2

Run01 (38652419070, B `942360b9`) returned **INVALID**, plus NO-GO under v1. That record stands:
`docs/perf/R20_PRF04_HEAP_REUSE_RUN01/` holds `judge_output.json`, `input.json.gz`, `summary.csv`, the verifier
output and the identity files. Two v1 rules did not measure the decision:
- **Absolute-arm linearity.** It failed identically in A, B and C0. Profiles attribute this to per-heap
  `flush_class_internal`, `drain_sidecar_ingress` and `trim_cold_retention` work, which falls as N grows (allocator
  state shared by every arm). A second cause was an L1 harness front-drain memmove (`memcpy` 137 → 101 → 0 Ir).
- **Absolute per-work allowances on L1.** For example +4 Ir on a cold claim that costs about 35 400 Ir. L1 sits below
  the shipping layer, and the 5% relative bound was applied to a 29-Ir bare lease release.

On 2026-10-15 the owner asked for the protocol to be improved rather than kept as it was. v2 changes:
- the price is gated at L2 at 1% of the same A window;
- linearity is gated on the B−A delta;
- the L1 and L2 harnesses drain the vector tail;
- the bench-only claim-source counter is branch-free.

The preregistration (§7) discloses that v2 was written after run01, and that v2 applied offline to run01 gives GO.
The deciding evidence is this fresh official run02 with the re-frozen candidate, not run01.

Run01 → run02 on the same cells:
- L1 cold went from +10 to +6 Ir per claim, the branch-free counter;
- L2 cold went from +9 to +7;
- L1 recycled went from +7 to +3 per pair;
- L1 wave_release arm linearity went from 43 to 0, the tail drain.

## 3. Reading

- **Mechanism ran as labelled.** The judge hard-asserts the activation oracle. B's wave take hits are positive and
  its recycled take hits are zero. Spill-release shows a positive net bit delta in its release-only window. Cold
  shows no take. `config_conflicts_total` delta is 0. A emits `observer=false` with null candidate counters.
- **Benefit.** In barrier waves B reuses FREE heaps instead of materialising new ones. Per wave window B costs
  2.3% of A at L1 and 10–11% of A at L2.
- **Price.**
  - Every claim pays a summary-word check: +6 Ir at L1, +7 at L2, where a cold thread start costs about 49 000 Ir
    at L2.
  - Every release pays the spill publish (one `swap` plus two `lock or`): +12 to +13 Ir.
  - All of these are well under 1% of the L2 lifecycle window.
  - The cost is constant per operation: delta linearity is 0 everywhere.
- **Not measured.** Native latency, contention and RSS; multi-socket behaviour. The idle-commit and p99 wins of the
  earlier V4 native runs (item 87) are not re-claimed here.

## 4. Decision

GO under the frozen v2 rules. PR #28 merges as the default claim path (`perf(runtime)`). The `production` feature
composition is unchanged: ReuseFirst is not a flag.
