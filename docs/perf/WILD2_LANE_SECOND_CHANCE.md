# WILD-2 — the own-free lane's second chance after a memo miss (`perf(runtime)` candidate, verdict HOLD for the owner)

**Class:** `perf(runtime)` candidate, **not merged**. The change touches `production` code (the cold exit of the
own-thread small-free lane) but not its memo-hit path. The preregistered gates (`docs/perf/wild2/PREREG.md`) are
partly met and partly unreadable on this machine; by the preregistration the decision goes to the owner. Tables and
the summary CSV are generated by `scripts/wild2_report.mjs` from `docs/perf/wild2/`; the prose cites them.

## 0. Summary

1. **What changed.** On a memo mismatch the lane now calls a cold function that probes the direct-mapped
   `own_cache`; an exact-base hit arms the memo and runs the lane's own acceptance steps (kind and payload start,
   granule bound, state byte, magazine push or the existing full-magazine tail). Everything else, and every reject,
   is the unchanged chain. `OWN_CACHE_SIZE` stays 16; the memo-hit path is not edited.
2. **It does what it was meant to.** Path activation (T3): in `wild` the second chance accepts 2 424 451 frees, 63.7 %
   of the 3 804 795 memo exits of the base probe (threshold 40 %). Deterministic instructions (callgrind, Linux,
   `wild` at scale 0.3): the memo-miss frees cost 25 495 319 Ir in base and 20 719 427 Ir in x (-18.7 %); the whole
   run is 2.77 % cheaper (168 176 393 against 163 519 983). `phase` and `burst`, where the memo almost never
   misses, change by +0.0005 % and +0.002 %.
3. **Linux judge, the target (T2).** Campaign 1 (12 blocks x 3 repeats): `wild` 0.972 (0.941-1.004), upper bound
   not below 1.0, FAIL by a hair. Confirmation campaign (24 blocks x 5 repeats, Amendment 2): **`wild` 0.963
   (0.941-0.986), A/A 0.999 (0.973-1.026): T2 PASS.** All Linux controls of the confirmation campaign are within
   the gate (`growmix` mean 1.029, upper bound 1.078, is the closest; callgrind Ir of `growmix` is -0.64 %).
4. **Windows judge, the primary target (T1): unreadable.** `wild` 0.938 (0.856-1.028), but the A/A control of the
   same campaign excludes 1 (0.939, 0.889-0.992); a pilot at HIGH priority was no quieter (A/A 0.916,
   0.790-1.062). Two Windows controls read KILL by the letter of C1 (`phase` 1.083, `burst` 1.030) with intervals that
   include 1 and A/A offsets of the same size; the hit path they exercise is instruction-identical (item 6).
5. **`bench:table` and MT controls (C3, C4) are noise-limited on this machine.** Set s1 reads GO, set s2 NO-GO
   through one cell (`direct 64 B`, +13.4 %) that flips by tens of percent between two runs of the same build; MT
   cells carry samples of 2-7 Mops/s against about 50. They are marked UNREADABLE by the post hoc noise rules of
   Amendment 1, not passed.
6. **Memo-hit path unchanged (C6).** The disassembly of the judge shim that inlines the lane differs between base
   and x only by the function address and one extra cold tail-jump stub after the `ret`.
7. **Not done / open:** CI of the PR (the evidence-registry row R-Ph3b must be re-verified on CI after the final
   code), `OWN_CACHE_SIZE` (the other half of the WILD-1 finding: 29 % of the lane frees in `wild` still reach the hash
   probe; 64 entries do not fit the `HeapCore` size assert under `--all-features`).

  process list taken during the Windows work showed the WSL VM, Defender and several other sessions using the CPU.

  the `__rust_alloc` boundary, one arm per process; MT is `malloc_macro`; `bench:table` is
  `scripts/bench-table-paired.mjs`.
- **Judge statistic.** Per block and arm the minimum of the repeats of `ns_per_op`; per block d = ln(x / base) and
  the A/A control ln(base2 / base), base2 a byte-identical copy of base; cell = exp(mean d) with a two-sided 95 %
  t-interval; a scenario is read only if its A/A interval includes 1. The rules T1, T2, T3, C1-C7 are those of
  `PREREG.md` section 3, applied by the script; Amendments 1 and 2 (written before the data they govern) are
  sections 6 and 7 of that file.
- **What the preregistration did not foresee.** The noise of this machine: see items 4 and 5 above and the
  amendments. Campaign 1 is reported in full and not replaced by campaign 2.

resolve effects of this size on a loaded shared machine.

## 4. What is left before this could merge

- **Evidence registry.** CI marks the row R-Ph3b stale because `dealloc_fast.rs` changed; it must be re-verified
  on CI (the `gate-run` mechanism) once the code is final, then the temporary config and label removed.
- **Owner decision** on the HOLD: merge as a small `perf(runtime)` gain with the limits above, or reject.
- **Not part of this change:** a larger `own_cache`. In `wild` 29 % of the lane frees still pass through the hash
  probe in the chain; with 64 entries that falls to 4.9 % (WILD-1 probe), but 64 entries exceed the `HeapCore`
  size assert under `--all-features` (9208 B measured, limit 9216 B) and would need a stack-budget decision or an
  out-of-line cache. `growmix` (1 382 444 hash probes against 73 015 second-chance accepts) is where that matters.

## 5. Limits

- One machine, shared; the Windows numbers of this report are noise-limited and say so; Linux (WSL2) numbers come
  from a different compiler than the Windows ones.
- The noise rules for C3 and C4 (Amendment 1 point 4) were written after seeing campaign 1 and are post hoc.
- `regrow` looked like a gain in campaign 1 on both platforms (0.911 and 0.937) and reads neutral in the Linux
  confirmation campaign (1.003, 0.948-1.061); its deterministic Ir is -1.41 %. It is not claimed.
- The WILD-1 report cites the callgrind per-call cost (91.7 Ir) from Linux at scale 0.3 next to the 88.8 % memo-miss
  rate from Windows at scale 1; the miss rate at the callgrind scale on Linux is lower (278 028 entries of the
  chain), which this report's per-function table (entries counted from the entry instruction) makes explicit.
