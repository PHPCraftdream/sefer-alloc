# DZ-3 — the foreign-free route pin cache becomes direct-mapped (`perf(runtime)`)

**Class:** `perf(runtime)` — the per-thread route pin cache of the foreign-free path
(`src/registry/heap_core_xthread/route_pin_cache.rs`) changes from 4 slots with round-robin eviction to 32
direct-mapped slots; the hit path stays instruction-identical, the miss path is not touched. Tables and the summary CSV
are generated by `scripts/dz3_report.mjs` from `docs/perf/dz3/`; the prose cites them. Platform: Linux (WSL2) only.

## 0. Summary

1. **The loss (PC-1 / PC-2).** A producer that frees blocks of one owner out of 5 or more segments in a non-sequential
   order missed the 4-slot cache on every free (cyclic order: hit rate 0.00 at m = 5, 8, 16 segments; random order
   0.79 / 0.50 / 0.25). A miss costs a directory lookup under a shard lock, a reference-count RMW and two sweeps:
   about 440-460 extra Ir per free (extra Ir per free over the sequential order of the same run: 446 -> 8 at m = 5 cyclic).
   Against mimalloc the foreign free of the base was 1.6-2.9 times slower in those orders (m = 5 ... 64), while
   the sequential order was 2.7-3.3 times faster than mimalloc.
2. **The change.** 32 direct-mapped slots indexed by `(base / SEGMENT) & 31`: consecutive 4 MiB segments of one owner
   fall into distinct slots, a lookup is one index and one compare (no scan), a miss evicts the slot's occupant. The
   sweep of dead (unlinked) pins is bounded to 4 occupied slots per call from a persistent cursor (it examines every
   occupied slot of a cache that holds at most 4 pins). The ghost events of the pin ledger stay at the same sites.
3. **Result (Linux, section A).** For m = 4 ... 16 segments every order reaches a hit rate of at least 0.99, i.e. only the first touch of each segment misses (counting build, asserted by the script).
   Time per foreign free, base -> change (mimalloc): m = 5 cyclic 74.0 -> 29.4 ns (mimalloc 40.6); m = 16 cyclic 83.5 -> 30.8 ns (mimalloc 48.6); m = 16 random
   104.8 -> 37.3 ns (mimalloc 44.9); m = 8 random 78.2 -> 38.9 ns (mimalloc 36.8). The sequential order moves by 1.00-1.06 of base in time
   (m = 16: 18.8 -> 19.9 ns (mimalloc 61.9)) and by 0.23 % (largest over m, 9776487 / 9782024; 11465575 / 11456345; 16590641 / 16602451; 30755297 / 30827274; 117833235 / 117781275) in program instructions.
4. **What is not fixed.** At m = 64 the 32 slots thrash like the 4 did: cyclic 89.4 -> 101.8 ns (mimalloc 43.0), random 169.9 -> 163.7 ns (mimalloc 57.8) (hit rate
   random 0.06 -> 0.25); the change is within the run-to-run spread there (cyclic, change / base: 0.97 in section C and 1.14 in
   section A, the same cell in two runs) and far above mimalloc. A cache that large would raise the retention bound further (section 3) or needs a
   different structure.
5. **Controls.** larson / mstress at T = 2 and T = 4 (section D): no difference readable (ranges overlap in every cell).
   The program instructions of the sequential order differ by under 0.3 % (section 0 item 3); the deterministic CI judge (`npm run iai`) runs on CI.
6. **Cost.** The retention bound of dead descriptors held by an idle thread grows with the slot count: 4 slots held up
   to about 1.16 MiB, 32 slots hold up to about 9.3 MiB per thread in the worst case (every class in use; typically far
   less; section 3). It is one constant (`PIN_CACHE_SLOTS`); 16 slots lose the m = 16 cyclic case (section B).

## 1. What was measured

- **Identity.** Base: `main` @ `6b8dd8e3` (the pin cache is identical to `c0ee7dfb`). Change: git tree
  `f81db41b431d18898b71e885e68852c76a1e5e40` (the working tree of branch `g1-dz3` at measurement time), recorded by
  `git write-tree` before the run; binary hashes, rustc and tool versions: `final_ident.txt`. The measured probe is
  `examples/g1_rhythm_probe.rs pincycle <m> <order> [mi]` (the `mi` arm was added for this report; the measured code
  path of the sefer arm is unchanged). Time builds use `production`; counting builds use
  `production bench-internals internals alloc-stats` (pin cache hits / misses). rustc 1.98.1, Linux 6.18 (WSL2),
  i7-11800H, shared with other sessions.
- **Entry point.** `SeferAlloc` as the `#[global_allocator]` of the probe process (the probe frees through
  `GlobalAlloc::dealloc` on another thread, the real foreign-free path `HeapCore::publish_foreign`); mimalloc through the
  same probe with `mi`.
- **Shape.** The owner allocates `(m + 2) x 4096` blocks of 1 KiB, `m` segments of 2048 freed blocks each are chosen; one
  producer thread frees them in order `seq` (segment by segment), `cyclic` (one block per segment in turn) or `random`
  (fixed seed). Time per free = producer wall time / (m x 2048).
- **Statistics.** Time: medians of 9 interleaved processes per arm and point. Hit rate: counting build, every point
  twice, asserted equal. Extra Ir per free: callgrind program totals, `(total(order) - total(seq)) / (m x 2048)` of the
  same arm (the set-up is identical across orders). MT controls: medians of 7 interleaved runs.

## 3. Reading

- **Why direct-mapped.** The first prototypes kept the linear search: 16 or 64 slots fix the cyclic and random orders but
  make every hit scan the array (the sequential order is slower than the 4-slot base at m = 16, section B). A last-hit
  memo helped the sequential order but not the others. The direct-mapped index makes the hit path independent of the
  slot count.
- **Why 32.** Direct-mapped 16 loses at m = 16 cyclic (1.03 of base: two of the 16 segments of the probe collide), 32
  and 64 slots hold m = 16; 32 keeps the retention bound at about 9.3 MiB instead of 18.6 MiB. Collisions between
  segments whose indices are congruent modulo 32 remain possible; consecutive segments never collide.
- **Why a sweep budget of 4.** With a budget of 8 occupied slots per call, the thrash cases (m = 64) paid more per
  miss than the base (cyclic 1.21 of base, section C); 4 matches the base's cost per miss. The sweep must still reclaim
  a dead pin on the first miss after its route is unlinked when the cache holds few pins: the existing test
  `sweep_reclaims_unlinked_cached_pin_after_next_miss` pins that, and a cache with at most 4 pins is swept completely
  by every call.
- **The sequential order.** Program instructions differ by 0.23 % (largest over m, 9776487 / 9782024; 11465575 / 11456345; 16590641 / 16602451; 30755297 / 30827274; 117833235 / 117781275); the time moves by 1.00-1.06 of base, which is
  noise plus cache-line effects of a 768 B thread-local array against 96 B. It is the only cost visible on the hit
  path.
- **Retention.** A cached pin keeps the route descriptor and its sidecar alive after the segment's reservation is gone;
  the descriptor of a segment with every class in use is about 0.29 MiB, so 32 slots bound the dead descriptors an idle
  thread holds at about 9.3 MiB (4 slots: about 1.16 MiB). The sweep reclaims dead pins on the next miss or insert of that
  thread; a thread that never misses again keeps them until it exits, as before.
- **Safety.** Every site that stores or drops a pin keeps its ghost event (`pin-cache/held` +1 / -1 at the same
  places; the catalog counts per function are unchanged), so the loom ledger of `tests/loom_b4_r1_pin_cache.rs` still
  sees exactly one reference per occupied slot. The unlink fallback and the incarnation check of `publish_cached` are
  untouched.

## 4. Limits

- Linux only (WSL2), one machine shared with other sessions; the foreign-free path was not measured on Windows.
- m = 64 is not fixed and its cells are noisy (section A and section C differ by 0.17 in change / base on the
  same cell).
- Collisions: bases whose segment indices are congruent modulo 32 evict each other. The new test uses a bound that
  tolerates a few colliding pairs; a 4-slot cache has hit rate 0.00 on it.
- The MT controls (section D) are underpowered for a 1-2 % effect: ranges overlap in every cell.
- The prototypes of section B were measured in separate runs, each against the base of its own run.
