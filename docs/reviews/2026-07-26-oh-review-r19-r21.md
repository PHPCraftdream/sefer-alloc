### 1.4 R19-1 severity calibration (do not oversell it)

The commit message calls it "a real, exploitable UAF" and backs that with a
reproduced `STATUS_ACCESS_VIOLATION`. The reproduction is credible and the
fix is correct (§3.1). But two facts must travel with that claim or it will
be mis-cited later:

1. **The bug is unreachable in `production`.** Branch (A) only compiles when

   is already UB on the caller's side; `hardened`'s task-#25 promise is
   precisely "detect it and no-op", and the bug was that this one branch
   silently did the opposite. So the correct framing is *"`hardened` failed
   to keep its own advertised defensive promise on one reachable path"*, not
   *"sefer-alloc had a memory-safety hole"*.

Under plain `production` the very same fabricated free still really frees the
segment today — by design, and correctly so per the crate's stated posture.

## 2. What else could be sped up — independent assessment vs. `OPEN_ITEMS.md`

I read `docs/perf/OPEN_ITEMS.md` end-to-end and formed my own list first.
Broad agreement on the *content* of the [D]/[L] tiers; **two substantive
disagreements on the [A] tier**, plus items the index structurally cannot
see.

### 2.1 Disagreement 1 — item 1 (OPT-H / in-place medium grow) should be closed, not left "Active" with a third-harness suggestion

`OPEN_ITEMS.md:70-73` closes the R21-2 update with: *"A genuinely
un-promoted, walks-the-Small-ladder-without-crossing-into-Large harness is
the one remaining unexplored variant if this lever is revisited; not yet
built, not scoped."* R21-2 §5 offers "two honest readings" and leaves the
question open.

**It is not open. The arithmetic closes it, and neither R20-3 nor R21-2 did
that arithmetic.**

OPT-H's precondition 3 forces `off` to be a carve position, i.e. a multiple
of `block_size(old_class)` (`carve_block` does `align_up(bump, block_size)`,
`alloc_core_small.rs:1439`). Precondition 4 additionally forces
`off % block_size(new_class) == 0`. Both together mean:

> `off` must be a multiple of `lcm(block_size(old_class), block_size(new_class))`,
> **and** that block must simultaneously be the segment's bump tail, **and**
> `off + block_size(new_class) <= SEGMENT` (4 MiB).

For the six `medium-classes` rungs (256 / 320 / 384 / 512 / 768 / 1024 KiB —
`size_classes.rs:106-111`) the legal offsets per adjacent transition are:

| transition | lcm | legal offsets in a 4 MiB segment that also satisfy precondition 5 |
|---|---|---|
| 256K→320K | 1.25 MiB | 1.25 MiB, 2.5 MiB (3.75 MiB overflows) — **2** |
| 320K→384K | 1.875 MiB | 1.875 MiB — **1** |
| 384K→512K | 1.5 MiB | 1.5 MiB, 3.0 MiB — **2** |
| 512K→768K | 1.5 MiB | 1.5 MiB, 3.0 MiB — **2** |
| 768K→1M | 3 MiB | 3.0 MiB — **1** |

So across the *entire* medium ladder there are at most one or two legal
offsets per transition per segment, and the block must additionally be the
bump tail at exactly that offset. The observed 0/320 and 0/20 are not a
harness artifact; **they are what the size-class table's factorization
predicts.** Building a third medium-ladder harness cannot change this.

The one range where the arithmetic *is* friendly is the sub-16 KiB geometric
1.25× ladder: `lcm(b, b')/b = b'/gcd(b, b')` is typically 2–5 there
(e.g. 16→32 gives 2, i.e. 50 % of carve positions qualify; 4096→5120 gives 5,
i.e. 20 %), and a single hot buffer *is* the bump tail by construction after
its first grow. So a `Vec`-push-shaped 16 B→16 KiB harness would plausibly
show a 20–50 % Stage-1 hit rate. **But that is also the range where the
move-leg is cheapest** (`realloc_grow_geometric` is already ~40× faster than
mimalloc per README:639), so the payoff is small.

**Recommended disposition:** move item 1 from **[A]** to **[L]** with the
above arithmetic recorded as the closing evidence, and — since the counters
are already committed and cost nothing — file the small-ladder Stage-1 re-run
as an optional one-hour probe rather than as "the one remaining unexplored
variant" of an active high-value lever. Leaving it in [A] guarantees another
round spends P1 budget on it.

### 2.2 Disagreement 2 — item 1's real question is being avoided

Three consecutive rounds have now measured this lever and returned
NULL/NO-GO (R18-2 re-run: still ~1,180×; R20-2: NULL; R21-2: NO-GO). The
question that is never on the queue is the one that actually matters:
**should `medium-classes` ship at all, or should it be formally rejected /
restricted to non-realloc workloads?** It is not in `production`, it has an
un-clearable realloc kill-gate, and it keeps generating work. A one-task

### 2.3 Levers the index does not contain, ranked by my own estimate

1. **The promotion `memcpy` itself, attacked by remapping rather than
   copying.** R20-3 §0 correctly identifies the cost as "the copy that moves
   the payload out of the medium segment". Every explored lever
   (`large-reserved-capacity`, `large-cache-extended`, OPT-H) attacks the
   *destination* or the *carve*, never the copy. The untried option is
   OS-level: Windows 10+ placeholder VA (`VirtualAlloc2`/`MapViewOfFile3`
   with `MEM_REPLACE_PLACEHOLDER`) and Linux `mremap` can move a ≥256 KiB
   page-aligned region without touching bytes. `crates/vmem` already owns the
   whole reservation surface. This is the single biggest unexplored idea for
   the one gate the project cannot clear. It is real work (per-OS, alignment
   constraints, interaction with `SegmentTable` identity), but it is the only
   idea I can find that changes the asymptotics rather than the constant.
2. **`contains_base` on the free hot path.** `dealloc_routing`
   (`heap_core_xthread.rs:771`) does an open-addressing hash probe into the
   heap's own `SegmentTable` on *every* free before anything else. mimalloc's
   equivalent is a pointer mask plus one header field read. A well-designed
   A/B here (header `owner_thread_free` compare vs. table probe) is exactly
   the kind of thing the existing iai gate resolves deterministically, and it
   sits on the hottest path the crate has. I do not see it anywhere in
   `OPEN_ITEMS.md` or in `PERF_PLAN_beat_mimalloc_small_medium.md`'s named
   eurekas. Cold 16 B is still ~2.37× mimalloc (README:765) and this is a
   plausible slice of it.
3. **Item 2 (mimalloc `Ir` arm) is correctly ranked but has been "feasible,
   not implemented" for a full round.** R20-4 is a good report and its §8
   sketch is concrete. It is a ~half-day task that would settle a
   ten-round-old argument. It should be *scheduled*, not re-assessed.

### 2.4 Where I agree with the index

The [D] tier (items 3–6) and [L] tier (7–11) match my own reading of the
underlying reports: each has an explicit victim/trigger and none has one
today. The index's discipline — closure trail, tier key, "never silently
ignore" convention — is genuinely good and is doing its job (R19-2, R20-1,
R21-2 all moved items through it correctly).

  would otherwise have run. ✔

  `layout.size().max(MIN_BLOCK) != large_size`: shrink-realloc of a promoted
  block declines OPT-G and takes the move leg, freeing the old block with the
  matching `old_layout`; a genuine Large allocation whose layout classifies
  `Some(c)` cannot exist except via promotion, because `class_for` is a pure
  function of `(size, align)` and `AllocCore::alloc` routes Large exactly
  when it returns `None`. I could not construct a false positive. ✔

Consequently `tests/regression_hardened_large_kind_own_free.rs`'s two new

**[M] Finding 3.1.2 — `large_layout_consistent`'s documented caller contract
was not updated for its second caller.**

`layout_consistent.rs:50-53` states: *"`base` MUST already be confirmed live
(`magic_at(base) == SEGMENT_MAGIC`) and `SegmentKind::Large` by the caller."*
The new call site satisfies liveness by a *different* argument —

violation gives an integrator no signal that their code is broken. A

**[H] Finding 3.2.1 — the new test's negative case does not actually protect
precondition 3, and its own assertion message claims that it does.**

`tests/r21_2_opt_h_stage1_precondition_probe.rs:279-288` asserts hits do not
increment for `objs[0]` and explains the failure as *"An increment would mean
the precondition logic is not actually checking tail-adjacency (a vacuously-
true check)"*. But `objs[0]`'s offset is `768 KiB = 786432`, and
`786432 % 1048576 != 0`, so **precondition 4 blocks it independently of
precondition 3**. Deleting `tail_adjacent` from the conjunction at
`alloc_core.rs:1976` leaves the whole suite green.

This is not my inference — the commit message states it as an observed fact:
*"forced tail_adjacent=true alone -- did NOT flip the negative test (both
tests still passed)"*. The author then ran a second, manual counterfactual
(forcing `new_class_aligned = true` in isolation) that *did* establish
precondition 3's discriminating power — **and that counterfactual exists
nowhere in the repository.** The committed suite's actual mutation-detection
coverage is: preconditions {3 ∧ 4} jointly, precondition 5 not at all,
preconditions 3 and 4 individually not at all.

Note also that `R21_2_OPT_H_STAGE1_HIT_RATE.md:82-87` describes the
non-vacuity check as forcing *both* flags at once — which is the weaker
experiment — while the commit message describes the stronger pair. The two
records of the same verification disagree.

**Recommended fix:** add a third scenario whose offset is `new_class`-aligned
but *not* tail-adjacent (e.g. carve the 768 KiB tail block, then carve one
more small block after it so `bump` advances past it, then grow the 768 KiB
block). That isolates precondition 3 and encodes the manual counterfactual
permanently. This matters more than it looks: the stated purpose of these
counters is to justify (or not) a future mechanism that, if built on a
silently-broken tail-adjacency check, *corrupts a live neighbouring block* —
the test's own words.

I traced it and believe it still holds: metadata stays below 768 KiB so the

**[L] Finding 3.2.4 — the design's precondition-4 justification is
incomplete, in a way that matters if OPT-H is ever revisited.**

`R20_3_INPLACE_MEDIUM_GROW_DESIGN.md` §2.1 justifies precondition 4 by
BinTable free-list reuse plus the `hardened` generation table. It does not
mention the two **unconditional** (not `hardened`-gated) offset checks on the
cross-thread reclaim path: `alloc_core_small_reclaim.rs:104` and `:241`, both
`if !(off as u32).is_multiple_of(bs) { return false; }`. Those are actually
the *strongest* reason precondition 4 cannot be relaxed: an OPT-H-grown block
at a non-`new_class`-aligned offset, freed from another thread, would be
silently dropped by `reclaim_offset` and leak permanently — the #114/#130
leak-to-abort class. The design's "every input OPT-H needs already exists /
nothing else in the system cares" argument (§2.2) is therefore
under-verified, even though its conclusion happens to be right.

## 4. Project-level improvements

### 4.1 `OPEN_ITEMS.md`'s scope rule is losing the highest-value items

The index's own §Scope says it *"covers `docs/perf/*.md` only... NOT a
general issue tracker — code TODO/FIXME comments, roadmap wishes, and
`docs/reviews/*` plan items are out of scope unless a perf gate report
explicitly flags them."*

R19-1's commit message flags **two** follow-ups in its own body:

1. `canary_survives_promotion_and_free_leaves_no_leak`
   (`tests/r14_4_promotion_free_correctness.rs`) is **flaky** — "failed 1 of
   3 runs" on pristine pre-fix code — "unrelated to this fix, left as a
   follow-up".
2. `clippy --features "hardened medium-classes" -D warnings` produces
   **11 dead-code errors**, present identically on pristine code, "that combo
   is simply outside today's CI feature matrix, also a follow-up".

Neither appears in `OPEN_ITEMS.md`, in `CHANGELOG.md`, or anywhere else
durable. I grepped. They exist only in one commit message body. **This is
verbatim the failure mode `OPEN_ITEMS.md` was created to prevent** (R18-8's
"the in-session TaskList does not survive a session boundary"), and the scope
rule is what let them through: a flagged-open item that originates in a
`fix(...)` commit rather than in a `docs/perf/*.md` report has nowhere to go.

A flaky test in the promotion-correctness suite is not a minor item — it is
the exact suite that would catch a regression of R19-1.

**Recommendation:** widen the scope to "any item a round explicitly defers,
whatever artifact flagged it", add a `[C]` (correctness/CI debt) tier, and
backfill these two.

### 4.2 A design's measurement instrument should be validated against the
design's own preconditions before the round that uses it

R20-3 §6.1 asked for a single-hot-buffer harness. R21-1 built one whose base
size is `MEDIUM_REALLOC_PROMOTION_THRESHOLD` itself, so the buffer promotes
to Large on grow #1 of every round and OPT-H's code path is reached once per
round at a fixed, precondition-4-hostile offset. R21-2 then spent its
analysis budget root-causing exactly that. Both the design (which specified
the harness) and R21-1's zero-trust review missed it, even though R21-1's own
module doc states the promoting behaviour explicitly and calls it
"intentional".

**Recommendation:** when a design doc specifies a measurement instrument, the
task that builds it should close with a one-paragraph "does this instrument
satisfy the mechanism's own preconditions?" check — for OPT-H that would have
been "can precondition 4 ever hold at the offsets this harness produces?",
answerable in five minutes of arithmetic (§2.1) and would have saved a round.

### 4.3 The CI feature matrix does not cover the configurations the round's
own work targets

Rounds 19–21 produced code and tests for `hardened + medium-classes`
(R19-1, never in CI) and for `medium-classes + alloc-stats` (R21-2, in CI
only via `--all-features`, never run there by the author). The
`feature-powerset` weekly job type-checks but does not test and does not use
`-D warnings`. When a round's flagship fix lands in a combination CI cannot
execute, the zero-trust discipline is carrying the entire regression burden
on one machine, once.

**Recommendation:** make it a rule that *if a task adds a test gated on a
feature combination, the same PR adds the CI row that runs it, or records in
`OPEN_ITEMS.md` why it cannot.*

### 4.4 A measurement report that names raw evidence should ship it —
R21-2 did not, R20-2 did

CLAUDE.md requires committed raw logs for cited evidence and a companion
machine-readable summary for perf-gate reports. R20-2 complies exactly
(`_raw_r20_2_*.log` ×4 + `..._summary.csv`). R21-2 reports concrete numbers
(0/320, 0/20) as its decision basis but commits **no** raw output and **no**
summary CSV — only reconstruction instructions for a throwaway probe binary
that was "not committed", plus a hand-traced offset dump (§4) whose provenance
is "a hand-traced repro, not part of the permanent test suite". R21-1's driver
config is explicitly gitignored. A future round cannot reproduce the 0/320
without rebuilding the probe from prose.

The verdict is NO-GO so nothing depends on it *today*; the point is the
precedent. Either R21-2 counts as a gate report (then it owes logs + CSV) or
it does not (then the rule needs a stated boundary). Right now it is neither.

### 4.5 Two stale statements landed *inside this same wave*

Rounds 19–21 spent four commits (R19-2, R19-3, R19-4, R20-1) repairing stale
documentation, and then introduced two more:

1. `docs/perf/R21_2_OPT_H_STAGE1_HIT_RATE.md:322-323` — the committed file
   ends with *"**Not committed, not pushed** — per this round's explicit
   instruction, this diff awaits a separate zero-trust review pass before any
   commit."* It is committed, as `b6af12d`. §"Base revision" likewise
   describes the diff as uncommitted. The pre-commit self-description was not
   updated at commit time.
2. `docs/perf/OPEN_ITEMS.md:173-174` — the R20-2 closure entry still reads
   *"The one remaining lever for R10-2's gate is unchanged: item 1 above
   (in-place medium-class grow), **still not designed**."* R20-3 designed it
   two commits later and R21-2 measured it. R21-2 updated item 1 but not the
   resolved-trail entry that contradicts it.

Both are exactly the "derived statement goes stale when the thing it derives
from moves" class that `docs/DESIGN_stale_literal_guard.md` was written for.
Neither is caught by `tests/no_stale_doc_references.rs`.

**Recommendation:** add a mechanical pre-commit check for the narrow, cheap
subset — a report that says "not committed" being committed is a one-line
grep, and it would have caught (1).

### 4.6 Positive observations, so the report is calibrated

- The zero-trust discipline is real and is doing work. R19-1 (revert-and-
  crash), R19-7 (inject-panic), R19-9 (wrong `EXPECTED_BYTES`) and R21-2

  the system working.
- `R20_2_...md` + its `_summary.csv` is a genuinely high-quality gate

- Willingness to publish NULL and NO-GO verdicts (R20-2, R21-2) with the

  should be and is worth preserving.
- R19-8's macro-design note documents *why the obvious two-arm design does
