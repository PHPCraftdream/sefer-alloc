## Verdict and boundary

The review's three source-level cost claims stand, with important limits. A Small magazine miss calls `drain_large_sidecar_ingress` in both ordinary and virgin-zeroed refill paths (`src/registry/heap_core/alloc/hot.rs:575,758`); the R10 active-kind index eliminates ghost-slot probes but still enumerates every active Large route (`src/alloc_core/alloc_core/sidecar_drain.rs:173-196`). K such misses and L stable active Large routes with no pending frees do K*L route inspections. This is not K*L Small bitmap cuts, a demonstrated latency regression, or a liveness/UB finding. The fixed `SmallSidecar` is 4096 pending `AtomicU64` words plus 262144 class `AtomicU8` entries, exactly 294912 bytes (288 KiB) per Small/Primordial route (`src/alloc_core/segment/remote_bitmap/sidecar_bitmap.rs:17-18`, `src/registry/segment_route/small_sidecar.rs:12-15`). `System.alloc_zeroed` at registration is an intentional, currently correct pre-issue storage/zeroing tradeoff (`src/registry/segment_route/directory.rs:53-57`), not by itself UB or a leak: descriptor and backing are freed after unlink and the last counted pin. The number is type/requested bytes, not measured RSS, OS commit, or allocator overhead. Finally, `scan_claimable_slot` precedes a capped fresh bump (`src/registry/heap_registry/stack.rs:13-33,88-102`); N sequential all-live first claims inspect sum(0..N-1) = N*(N-1)/2 prior occupied slots, 8,386,560 at N=4096. That is a cold logical-probe count, not measured startup time or a statement about every concurrent schedule.

The live contracts matter more than a clever hint. A foreign Small producer ends at its sidecar `fetch_or(AcqRel)`; a Large producer ends at the descriptor `LIVE -> PENDING` transition (`src/registry/segment_route/pin.rs`, `src/registry/segment_route/large_state.rs`). It touches neither reservation nor owner core afterward. `RoutePin` retains only independent descriptor/sidecar storage, while the outstanding issue credit prevents reservation release until owner retirement. The strict owner sweep fixes high-water and exchanges every covered Small word once, consuming every detached record before releasing a root (`src/alloc_core/alloc_core/sidecar_drain.rs:112-168`). `trim_for_recycle` uses it before TLS `LIVE -> FREE`; a winning re-claim trims before handing out the heap (`src/registry/heap_core/state/ownership.rs:105-125`, `src/global/tls_heap.rs:187-216`, `src/registry/heap_registry/claim.rs:278-289`). The R9 background `(slot,word)` cursor instead charges a finite ingress budget and revisits under a successful `FREE -> MAINTENANCE` lease or fallback try-lock; it is not the strict trim, and its other cold-retention stages are not globally budgeted. The R10 `ActiveKindIndex` holds exact owner-only membership, not pending state (`src/alloc_core/segment/segment_table/active_kind_index.rs`, `active_kind_ops.rs`). Keep these three mechanisms distinct.

README and `docs/REMOTE_FREE_SIDECAR_REVISION_2026-09-29.md` still mark terminal-sidecar release acceptance pending. The older phase-0 text in `docs/REMOTE_FREE_TERMINAL_PUBLICATION_CONTRACT.md` describes an intrusive prototype in places; its strict pre-entry and conditional ownerless promises are useful obligations, but source and the later sidecar revision determine the current mechanism. None of these P3 cost changes is a remedy for the separate R11 P2-1 experimental shard finding or the P4 findings.

## P3-1: bounded owner-only Large checks on Small refills

Recommendation: add a separate `drain_large_sidecar_ingress_hot_bounded(cursor, B)` used by the two Small magazine-refill siblings. `B` is a positive fixed number of actual active-Large route probes per refill, independent of L and table high-water; choose it from a measured reclaim-lag/retention versus refill-cost tradeoff, not by fiat. Retain the current full Large sweep on Large allocation/zeroed paths initially: those paths may depend on finding a released route/cache candidate before reserve. Retain `drain_sidecar_ingress` as a complete cold cut for explicit trim, TLS exit, and re-claim; retain R9's separate bounded background cursor and periodic service. This targets precisely the K*L Small-refill prelude without changing the terminal publication protocol.

Store only a numeric next-slot cursor in the exclusively owned heap (or core), never a root, `RoutePin`, iterator borrow, or bitmap snapshot. At each call use R10's `next_active(Large, from)`, re-read `base_at(index)` and validate kind before claim. Advance `from = index + 1` before reclaim can unregister the current route. Wrap once, visit any slot at most once in that call, and preserve the next numeric position across calls; do not restart at zero on every refill or on register/recycle churn. The index is exact for *registered* routes even when clean. A retained active Large instance whose publisher paused before terminal CAS remains registered by its credit. If the hot check misses it, later publication leaves `PENDING` for a later hot pass or cold sweep. Under repeated genuine Small misses, fair owner execution, and a continuously registered target, a monotone numeric cursor visits it in a finite number of passes; with stable L, at most roughly ceil(L/B)+1 misses from an arbitrary cursor position, and even under finite-index churn the 4096-slot domain gives a finite visit bound when the target remains registered. There is no guarantee from magazine hits alone (none exists now), no arbitrary paused-`OWNED` takeover, and no wall-clock deadline. A `FREE` heap's ownerless progress still requires successful service startup and available future leases; R9's background cursor supplies that independently of hot refills.

Do not create a false OOM at table pressure. Today's full prelude may retire a pending Large route before a Small refill needs a fresh segment slot. If a bounded prelude followed by `refill_class_bump[_virgin]` produces zero blocks, release any tcache borrow, do one full Large sweep, then retry that refill once before returning null. Also audit any direct Small path changed by this work for the same capacity failure. No loop-until-empty, no repeated full sweep on the ordinary no-pending miss. This rescue is cold, explicitly counted separately, and may help an OS-pressure failure too; it is not part of the per-successful-refill `B` bound. A partially successful refill already has usable blocks and can defer the remaining demand to its next refill. Keep the existing free-list/Small discovery and directory rescue behavior.

A pending-only index is not the cheap equivalent. If a producer marks a summary before terminal publication, pauses, and the owner clears that summary and cuts an empty bitmap, the producer's later terminal RMW has no remaining wakeup. A release/acquire annotation cannot repair that execution. A post-terminal summary requires a new completion point, retaining the independent descriptor pin through its last update, and proving no reservation access after bitmap publication; even an RMW-chain design adds a foreign-free RMW and needs overflow/late-publication analysis. A finite pending queue would additionally need lossless admission or a persistent overflow bit plus an independent full census: a full queue cannot silently drop the only notification for a terminally published instance. Its negative test must fill the queue, pause a publisher on both sides of terminal publication with barriers, overflow it, then prove exactly-once eventual retirement without another producer. Do not add an owner-core pointer, TLS access, producer allocation, or metadata write after the defined terminal last access for this cost fix. If measured hot-scan cost and reclaim lag later justify a pending index, it is a separate protocol review with a complete publication/clear/overflow/retirement proof, never just an extra flag.

Acceptance owed by a later implementation: count candidate inspections *before* each `base_at`/route attempt, and count full-rescue calls separately. With real `HeapCore` magazine misses at L=8 and L=64, no pending frees and the same K, successful refills should inspect at most K*B active Large routes, not K*L; confirm the magazine really emptied and the measured entry point is the `GlobalAlloc`/`HeapCore` chain, not only `AllocCore`. A persistent pending Large at a slot beyond the first B must retire after repeated Small-only misses; a `cursor=0` mutant must fail. Pause a producer before terminal CAS at a deterministic barrier, do a clean hot pass, publish, and show later retirement exactly once; include post-cut publication, a producer held after terminal CAS, and last-credit release. A strict trim begun after a synchronized terminal publication must still reclaim it in that trim; a mutant replacing strict with bounded must fail. At a logical full-table pressure fixture, a pending route beyond B must be recovered by the one-time rescue before Small allocation reports OOM. Use compact real reservations for integration plus a reduced logical table for 4095/4096 boundaries, not thousands of real segments or a fake GiB load. Regress `tests/r9_bounded_background_maintenance.rs`, the R10 active-kind census/late-publication controls, fallback, TLS exit, cache/reissue, and no-`alloc-decommit` paths. A no-pending `LARGE_SIDECAR_SLOT_INSPECTIONS` delta alone cannot prove reclamation or the pressure rescue.

## P3-2: adaptive class metadata, with fallible issue preparation

Recommendation: prototype a route-local uniform-leaf representation, not homogeneous 4 MiB segments and not a global pending notification. Keep the 32 KiB authoritative pending bitmap and its per-word `swap(0, AcqRel)` semantics. Partition the 4 MiB segment into, for example, 4 KiB leaves (256 16-byte granules). Each leaf has an encoded uniform class (zero means never issued) and a separately stored `AtomicPtr` to an optional 256-entry atomic-byte mixed leaf. On first issue set the uniform class; on the first different-class issue, the owner allocates a mixed leaf with `System`, fills every entry with the old uniform code, writes the new offset's code, then publishes the genuine allocation pointer. Thereafter owner issue updates only the offset's code. Producer publication still touches only the pending word and performs no allocation, class read, owner-core access, or extra RMW. Owner cut reads the selected code only after detaching a pending bit. All mutable class/leaf operations remain owner-only; producers can hold route pins concurrently but cannot read these fields. Do not demote a mixed leaf while a route exists; unlink and last-pin Drop free its backing. Pooled Small routes retain their leaf state; released/reissued routes start with fresh descriptor incarnation and fresh storage.

This is a candidate, not a release threshold. On a 64-bit target the illustrative 4 KiB geometry has 32768 pending bytes + 1024 uniform bytes + 1024*8 leaf-pointer bytes = 41984 bytes (41 KiB) before `Entry`, alignment and allocator overhead, plus 256 bytes per mixed leaf. If all 1024 leaves become mixed, logical class+bitmap storage is 297 KiB, *worse* than today's 288 KiB, and up to 1024 System allocations add overhead. A 16 KiB leaf reduces the pointer baseline but makes a whole 1 KiB leaf mixed after one class conflict; its crossover must be measured. A fixed six-bit packed class map is a simpler bounded alternative, but 58 current maximum classes need six bits and the theoretical 192 KiB class bits still leave 32 KiB pending (224 KiB total); word-local atomic packing/padding may cost more and save too little to justify bitfield complexity. A whole-segment one-class policy can slash metadata but changes fragmentation and active-segment count radically. Compare uniform/mixed actual distributions, syscall count, zeroing, RSS/commit, cache misses and issue/cut CPU before choosing. The review's `<=64 KiB` was an *example budget*, not a universal contract; a release gate needs a workload-backed budget and same-regime performance/retention evidence.

The hard part is not the leaf lookup; it is OOM before issue. Current `issue_small` is infallible and is called after free-list or bump/live-count mutations in scalar pop, batch drain, scalar carve, and batch carve (`src/alloc_core/small/alloc_core_small/alloc_core_small_impl.rs:367,486,659,821`; `src/alloc_core/segment/segment_table/segment_table_impl.rs:297-306`). A new mixed-leaf allocation must be prepared before any block is handed to a user or magazine. Refactor those four issue transactions to preflight every needed spill (including all leaves a batch crosses) before changing freelist head, allocation bitmap, bump, live credit, directory or output slots; or provide a fully proved rollback of every partially changed field. A routine `System` OOM should return the appropriate null/partial-refill result while preserving the still-owned block and retryability, not abort from `GlobalAlloc`. The register-time sidecar and descriptor allocations remain fallible prepare-before-route-publication; primordial attach failure must still roll back. No `Box`, `Vec`, or installed-`GlobalAlloc` recursion is allowed in these allocator paths. Do not accidentally allocate a mixed leaf during foreign deallocation or owner cut.

The pointer/provenance and ordering proof is explicit: keep the leaf's original `System` pointer in `AtomicPtr`, not an integer-cast dereference or a forged pointer from the numeric route key. A separate uniform code avoids pointer tagging. If tagging is later proposed, its real base pointer, alignment bits, `with_addr`/`map_addr` reconstruction, and exact deallocation pointer must all be proved; `without_provenance` is only a numeric key, never a dereference capability. Initialize all atomic objects before route publication. Owner issue's class write precedes allocation handoff; producer's terminal `fetch_or(AcqRel)` and owner `swap(AcqRel)` carry the class visibility already required by the current bitmap protocol. A mixed pointer is published only after its contents are initialized; owner loads it with Acquire and never forms a broad `&mut` alias over producer-reachable pending atoms. Offset is still a 16-byte granule inside the *issued* high-water; encoded class is still 1..`SMALL_CLASS_COUNT`; `BitmapCut::pop` must reject invalid/zero codes before discarding a credit, and `reclaim_sidecar_record` must retain payload-start, class-size, bump, free/magazine and credit guards. A changed class at an offset is legal only after that prior allocation's credit was retired. Route unlink closes new pin admission; last `EntryHandle` drop releases all mixed leaves, base sidecar and descriptor with their exact System layouts, possibly after reservation release. Do not read a sidecar through a retained owner borrow after its route is removed.

Acceptance owed: a pointer-free independent route-byte/accounting oracle at the `System` seam records requested `alloc`, `alloc_zeroed`, free bytes and allocation count by route; a separate cold route census establishes the number and kind of live descriptors. Check both the initial zeroed byte request and later mixed-leaf initialization, and check allocated-minus-freed bytes return to zero after unlink and last pin, including paused post-terminal pin release. `size_of::<SmallSidecar>() == itself` is no oracle. For real compact homogeneous, boundary-mixed and adversarial all-mixed fixtures, measure OS RSS/commit/reserved bytes and page faults separately from logical requested bytes; `System.alloc_zeroed` may use lazy zero pages. Include Primordial, minimum requested sizes 1..7, narrow reborrows, scalar and batch issue, class changes after recycle/reissue, pool retention, same-VA new route, generation, failure injection at register/spill/batch preflight, and no-global-allocation reentrancy. A fake 1024-route allocation to obtain a headline is unnecessary: use a few valid routes and a checked formula only as extrapolation. Miri should cover actual pointer provenance, owner borrow scopes and Drop-after-last-pin; Loom should model publication/cut/credit and pointer-publication ordering, while native tests establish System/OS behavior. Mutants that overwrite a uniform code while another class is still live, read an uninitialized mixed leaf, or free it at unlink despite a pin must fail.

## P3-3: hint first, fresh bump while capacity remains, cold recovery

Recommendation: use the existing one-entry `reuse_hint` as an O(1) *candidate* first, then the capped CAS `bump_count` while `count < MAX_HEAPS`. Only after bump reports full (or on an explicit cold recovery following chunk/heap construction OOM) scan claimable slots. Keep `scan_claimable_slot` materialized-FREE-first, remembering unmaterialized indices only as a second choice. This removes the triangular probe work from an all-live first-touch wave without introducing a second global availability bitmap with its own publication/clear races. The `HeapSlot::state` CAS remains the only ownership authority: `EMPTY -> INITIALIZING`, `FREE -> LIVE` and `FREE -> MAINTENANCE` grant access only on `Ok`; every `Err` retries selection. The capped `count` CAS must never overshoot 4096. `initialised` Release/Acquire still gates all heap dereferences, and a recycled heap is strictly trimmed before returning its pointer. No claim hint may turn a `LIVE`, `INITIALIZING`, or `MAINTENANCE` slot into a second owner.

This is a deliberate policy trade: the latest available recycled slot keeps priority, but an overwritten older FREE hint may wait while fresh capacity exists. It is *temporarily deferred*, not silently lost: a full scan at cap finds materialized FREE or unmaterialized `EMPTY` indices, and a full cold scan after materialization failure can recover an existing FREE heap instead of returning a false resource failure. A minted index whose chunk failed to materialize remains below `count`, with no core to read; publish a retry hint and invalidate saturation, and allow a later cold scan to reattempt it. Do not repeatedly return the same OOM index if another materialized FREE slot exists: on that failure make one materialized-FREE recovery pass that excludes the failed candidate, then return null/fallback if no usable candidate exists. A constructor failure returns `INITIALIZING -> FREE` with `initialised=false` and advertises it for retry. Maintenance remains non-materializing and CAS-gated; its `MAINTENANCE -> FREE` release should also offer the O(1) hint, as well as invalidating saturation. Keep the existing TLS/fallback behavior when claim truly returns null and keep deallocation allocation-free. No scan on the normal fresh-first path, no slot construction in a stats/maintenance scan.

At capacity, a negative scan may be cached only from a stable full-count observation; a scan begun below cap cannot declare a newly full table saturated after missing concurrently minted `EMPTY`. Preserve the versioned `SaturationHint` protocol (`src/registry/bootstrap/saturation.rs`): availability publication after successful FREE transition invalidates a negative scan, and snapshot/CAS prevents a concurrent publication from being overwritten by a stale negative result. Its RMW read, not a plain stale negative load, checks a completed publication; disable on epoch exhaustion rather than wrap into an old snapshot. A delayed old `reuse_hint.store` may overwrite a newer candidate or point to a now-owned slot; consuming it via swap and failing the state CAS is safe, and cap/cold recovery retrieves any displaced FREE index. Treat `reuse_hint` as advisory even when it looks in-range. If the above temporary deferral causes unacceptable chunk materialization/RSS or config-conflict churn, add a *separate measured design* for an exact reusable-slot set (e.g. fixed-capacity, nonallocating free-index queue/bitmap with duplicate suppression and CAS validation). That design must prove delayed FREE publication, maintenance races, failed-CAS reinsertion, ABA/reissue, overflow and chunk-OOM retry; merely adding a summary bit is not a free improvement. A bounded rotating probe before fresh bump is another O(1)-per-claim option if measured deferral is the only problem. Do not assert strict global recycled-first selection from the proposed single-hint path.

Acceptance owed: instrument candidate `slot_if_materialised` probes, not just successful claims. For N simultaneously live first-touch claimants at N=16,32,64 in a deterministic selector model, probes before the fresh bump should be O(N) (ideally zero except hint validation), not the triangular N*(N-1)/2; revert-to-scan-first must fail. A compact real `GlobalAlloc` first-bind/barrier case confirms the model's path without materializing 4096 heaps. Test latest and overwritten FREE hints, delayed publish after another claim, lost CAS against `LIVE`/`MAINTENANCE`, FREE after lease release, constructor failure with `initialised=false`, unmaterialized minted index with injected chunk OOM and later retry, full-cap recovery, and true full/no-FREE saturation. At the logical 4095/4096 boundary assert no count overshoot and no unclaimed minted `EMPTY`/`FREE` index becomes permanently unreachable; check the final state against an independent cold census, not the hint itself. Model publication during the negative scan and near-epoch-exhaustion invalidation; mutants that trust the hint as authority, mark saturated from a pre-cap scan, omit a FREE publication, or repeatedly select one failed OOM index while another FREE slot exists must fail. On real runs measure claim latency *and* peak materialized chunks/commit under the same churn regime before accepting the fresh-first tradeoff.
