**No P1 finding exists.** I re-derived the primitive from scratch, without
leaning on the eight prior clean bills:

- **Write-site enumeration (exhaustive).** Every store/CAS to `self.ptr`:
  `null→sentinel` (claim CAS, `src/lib.rs:590-607`); `sentinel→real`
  (`:648`, by the sentinel owner, immediately after the release-active
  `assert!` at `:639`); `sentinel→null` (`:666`, and `RollbackGuard::drop` at
  `:407`, both by the sentinel owner); and `dbg_rollback_reenterable`'s two
  stores (`:806`, `:835`) — the first reachable only after that probe's own
  entry CAS (`:795-802`) proved the cell `null` and installed the probe's
  sentinel, the second additionally gated on `postcondition_holds`
  (`:832-834`). **No path ever writes a cell observed `READY`; `READY` is
  terminal, so there is no ABA.**
- **No sentinel collision reachable.** Both reserved addresses (`0`, `1`) are
  unreachable as published values: `is_ready` (`:483-486`) rejects both on
  read; the `assert!` at `:639` rejects both on publish, in every profile —
  and CI runs `cargo test -p racy-ptr-cell --release` specifically so that
  `init_returning_the_sentinel_address_panics` cannot pass vacuously if the
  check were demoted back to `debug_assert!`.
- **Happens-before.** The one load-bearing pair is the winner's `Release`
  publish (`:648`) against every reader's `Acquire` (`:500`, `:581`, `:680`).
  The loser's CAS *failure* ordering is `Relaxed`, which loses nothing: the
  failed load's value is discarded and the spin loop re-loads with `Acquire`.
- **Unwind window.** `RollbackGuard` is armed (`:616`) exactly across the one
  window in which the sentinel is held across caller code, and defused on both
  non-unwinding exits (`:651`, `:665`). Nothing fallible sits between the
  publish store and `defuse()`, and nothing fallible sits between `defuse()`
  and the OOM store.
- **Panic-inside-the-critical-section ordering.** Re-derived independently
  because it is the crate's sharpest real hazard: on an `assert!` failure the
  panic **hook runs before unwinding begins**, so the hook's allocations
  precede `RollbackGuard::drop`. Inside a `#[global_allocator]` that is a
  self-deadlock on the thread's own sentinel, not an abort. The crate's docs
  state exactly this (`:125-128`) and do **not** overclaim that the guard
  covers it (`:92-95`). Accurate.
- **Probe-vs-winner clobber.** Re-derived the interleaving families of
  `dbg_rollback_reenterable` against a concurrent `get_or_try_init` and against
  a second probe: in every one, the probe stores only while it provably owns
  the sentinel. Loom property 6 covers this and passes.
- **`init` arity.** `get_or_try_init` calls `init` **at most once per call**,
  including across the re-race path (every winner branch returns; only the
  loser branch loops). `FnOnce` would suffice — a known, non-breaking
  carryover, not a defect.
- **Constructor surface.** `new` and `Default::default` are the only ways to
  build a cell (fields private, no `Clone`/`From`), so the
  `align_of::<T>() >= 2` guard cannot be bypassed.
- **`unsafe` sites.** The three `NonNull::new_unchecked` calls (`:504`, `:586`,
  `:694`) are each preceded, one to three lines above, by a check proving `p`
  non-null. The unconditional `unsafe impl Send`/`Sync` (`:339`, `:341`) mirror
  `AtomicPtr<T>`'s own explicit impls, for the same reason (only a raw `*mut T`
  crosses; `PhantomData<*mut T>` is what removed the auto-impls).
- **Provenance.** The sentinel is `without_provenance_mut`-built and never
  dereferenced — re-confirmed under `-Zmiri-strict-provenance`.
- **Root shim drift.** `src/registry/bootstrap.rs:224-431` re-read against the
  current crate line by line: alignment guard, `RollbackGuard`, release-active
  `assert!`, spin-on-`INITIALIZING` rule, and the conditional final restore in
  `dbg_rollback_reenterable` are all present and behaviourally identical.
  **No drift.**

No UB, no lost happens-before, no ABA break, no reachable sentinel collision,
no double-init, no wedge. **That is the ninth consecutive clean bill on the

## 1. Findings

### F1 — **P3** — `src/lib.rs:149` says *"the cell-consistency guarantee **below**"*, but the only cell-consistency guarantee in the module docs is 57 lines **above** it; the README's byte-for-byte counterpart sentence says "above"

**Where:** `crates/racy-ptr-cell/src/lib.rs:146-149`. Counterpart:
`crates/racy-ptr-cell/README.md:114-117`. Referent:
`crates/racy-ptr-cell/src/lib.rs:90-95`.

The closing sentence of the `std` mitigation bullet reads:

> Note also that `panic = "abort"` compiles the crate's internal rollback guard
> out entirely (it is unwind-only) — under this profile the cell-consistency
> **guarantee below** comes from the process dying, not from the guard.

The guarantee it means is stated at `:92-95`, in the fourth `init` bullet:

> This crate's rollback guard keeps the CELL consistent across an unwinding
> `init` (the sentinel is rolled back, not left wedged), but it cannot make the
> unwind itself sound once the frame below is `GlobalAlloc::alloc`.

That is **above**, not below. Reading forward from `:149`, the module doc
contains `### Fork and signal safety` (`:151-174`), `## Sentinel encoding`
(`:178-189`), `## What the caller owns` (`:191-199`) and `## Portability limit`
(`:201-215`). **None of them states a cell-consistency guarantee**, and the
nearest candidate — the fork section — states the opposite (that a
sentinel-holding cell inherited across `fork()` is unrecoverable, with no reset
API).

The README carries the identical sentence at `:114-117` with the word
**"above"**, which is correct there. So the two published documents disagree on
the direction of the same cross-reference, and the rustdoc one — the docs.rs
page — is the wrong one.

**Provenance.** `git log -S "guarantee below" -- crates/racy-ptr-cell/src/lib.rs`
returns exactly one commit, `3edc477` (task #1378, cycle-6 F1's fix), so this
predates `de7da71` and was already mis-directed the day it landed; the section
reorder neither introduced nor repaired it. It appears in none of the eleven
prior reviews.

**Concrete failure scenario.** A `std` consumer reaches the end of the
`panic = "abort"` mitigation bullet, is told that under that profile some
"cell-consistency guarantee below" is carried by process death rather than by
the guard, and scrolls down to find which guarantee that is. The next thing
below is the fork/signal subsection, whose subject is a hazard the profile
choice has no bearing on; resolving the reference there produces a
non-sequitur. The reader either gives up on the sentence (losing the real
point — that `panic = "abort"` silently disables an advertised headline feature,
which is itself cycle-3 F3's finding) or concludes the docs are internally
inconsistent at the one place they are explaining a build-profile trade-off.

**Suggested fix.** One word, one file: `below` → `above` in
`src/lib.rs:149`, matching `README.md:117`. (A stronger version, if it is
being touched anyway, is to name the referent rather than gesture at it: "…the
cell-consistency property described in the fourth bullet above comes from the
process dying, not from the guard.")

### F2 — **P3** — `de7da71`'s section reorder invalidated the transitional sentence at `src/lib.rs:153`: *"The rules above are all about what `init` does"* now sits immediately after a subsection about the consumer's **link environment**, not about `init`

**Where:** `crates/racy-ptr-cell/src/lib.rs:153-155`, introduced into this
position by `de7da71`. `README.md` is unaffected (its fork block, `:62`, does
not carry this transition).

Before `de7da71`, the module doc read: five `init` obligation bullets
(`:79-100`) → `### Fork and signal safety`, opening with

> The rules above are all about what `init` *does*; two further hazards break
> the cell from **outside** `init`, with no misbehaving closure anywhere.

— a correct contrast, because "the rules above" were literally and exclusively
the five `init` bullets. `de7da71` moved the panic-site material between the
two, so the current order is:

```
:79-100   five `init` obligation bullets
:102      ### Panic sites and the two link environments
:104-149  the table + "Every row that reaches the panic runtime…" + two
          mitigation bullets (`#[panic_handler]`; `panic = "abort"` +
          `std::panic::set_hook`)
:151      ### Fork and signal safety
:153      "The rules above are all about what `init` *does*; …"
```

The subsection the sentence now reaches back over is not about what `init`
does. Its two mitigation bullets are instructions about the **binary's link
environment and profile** — supply a non-allocating `#[panic_handler]`; set
`panic = "abort"`; install a non-formatting `std::panic::set_hook` — none of
which is a rule `init` can keep or break. (Only the trailing "or, better, an
`init` that cannot panic at all" is.) So the universal quantifier in "are **all**
about what `init` does" is now false for the material immediately above it,
while remaining true of the bullet list two screens up.

**Why this is worth a line at all, given how small it is.** It is the specific
question this cycle was asked to check, and the answer is not "no". It is also
the sixth consecutive cycle in which the remediation of this section left a
referential residual behind — cycle 4→5→6→7 each found one at P2, and each was
introduced by the immediately preceding fix. `de7da71` broke that chain in the
sense that matters (nothing contract-bearing regressed, and the finding
severity fell from P2 to P3), but the *mechanism* — moving text without
re-checking the sentences that point at it — produced exactly one more instance.

**Concrete failure scenario.** A reader who takes the sentence at face value
concludes that the panic-site table and the two link-environment mitigations are
`init`-behaviour rules, i.e. things they can discharge by writing a
well-behaved closure. They are not: the `panic = "abort"` + non-allocating-hook
recipe is a **build configuration** the consumer owes regardless of how careful
their `init` is, and the crate's own text one screen up is emphatic that a
`std` consumer "needs `panic = "abort"` **and** a `std::panic::set_hook`". Being
mis-filed as an `init` rule is a small nudge in the direction of skipping it.

**Suggested fix.** Re-bind the transition to what it actually contrasts with,
without a positional word — e.g. `src/lib.rs:153`:

> The obligations on `init` above, and the panic mitigations that go with them,
> are all about code paths a caller controls; two further hazards break the cell
> from **outside** `init`, with no misbehaving closure anywhere.

Same class of fix as `de7da71`'s own F1 (prefer naming over positional
cross-reference), applied to the one sentence the reorder moved past.
