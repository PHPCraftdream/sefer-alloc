## 1. Did we actually make the code faster?

**Short answer: almost nothing in Rounds 13–17 is a confirmed `production`
speedup. The one candidate promotion (`class-aware-dirty`) has an unconfirmed
full-round wall-clock effect after four independent measurements; the real,
large, statistically solid wins from this era are all opt-in and not part of
`--features production`; and the one genuine "recovered more than the
regression" story (R17-3) is a bootstrap-time fix, not a per-op win.**

`Cargo.toml:399` — `production = ["alloc-global", "alloc-xthread",
"alloc-decommit", "fastbin", "alloc-segment-directory",
"primordial-lazy-commit", "class-aware-dirty"]` — has been **byte-identical
since Round 13** (confirmed explicitly in the Round 14/15/16/17 CHANGELOG
entries, e.g. `CHANGELOG.md:214-216`, `:318-319`, `:437-438`). No round in
13–17 changed this list.

### 1a. Real, confirmed production-path speedups with numbers from committed gate reports

- **`alloc-segment-directory`** joined `production` in **Round 8**
  (`ec7ac34`), before this review's window — it is background context, not a
  Round 13–17 achievement, but it is the one unambiguous, still-standing
  production win in the codebase's history (closes an O(D×49)/O(S)-shaped
  regression, `Cargo.toml:399-413`).
- **R17-3 (`b8612bc`, `CHANGELOG.md:53-76`)** — removed two `MAX_SEGMENTS`
  -scaled zero-fill loops from `bootstrap::primordial()`, gating them
  `cfg(miri)`-only. `npm run iai`'s 12-bench suite recovered **exactly
  −81,966 Ir** on every bench (`large_alloc_free_cycle 85,274 → 3,308 Ir`).
  This is a real, deterministic (iai/Callgrind, not wall-clock-noisy) number,
  and it is larger than R16-4's originally-measured regression (61,440 Ir) —
  see §1d below for why that distinction matters. It is a bootstrap-time
  constant, not a marginal per-op cost: the CHANGELOG explicitly notes
  "every bench's `Ir/op*` is unchanged" (`CHANGELOG.md:222-223`).
- **R17-4 (`1b761f4`, `CHANGELOG.md:77-108`)** — fixed a real 4 MiB
  Large-segment leak under `medium-classes` (misrouted dealloc dispatch in
  `HeapCore::dealloc_own_thread_with_base`, `src/registry/heap_core_free.rs`).
  This is the round's most significant correctness finding, and its own
  gate-doc addendum (`docs/perf/R14_4_MEDIUM_REALLOC_PROMOTION_GATE.md:150-190`)
  shows a striking RSS number (~1.0 GiB → ~68 MiB steady-state commit for the
  probe workload) — **but this fix, and the number, live entirely under the
  opt-in `medium-classes` feature**, not `production`. Under plain
  `production` the `#[cfg]` split compiles the fix's branch out entirely,
  confirmed by iai matching R17-3's post-fix numbers to the Ir
  (`CHANGELOG.md:106-108`). So this is a correctness fix with a large *opt-in*
  perf payoff, not a `production` speedup.

### 1b. Real, measured, opt-in speedups that are NOT in `production`

These are the large, statistically solid numbers Rounds 12–14 produced — and
none of them ship by default:

- `exact-span-large` (R12-3): RSS amplification 15.78×→1.05× at 260 KiB down
  to essentially 1.00–1.05× across the whole 260 KiB–4 MiB range
  (`docs/perf/R12_13_PAGE_RUN_LAYER_DEFERRED.md:88-98`). Opt-in only.
- `large-reserved-capacity` growth-factor fix (R14-6, `8265b1c`): reversed
  R13-6's own +102.3% Ir / +52.7% Estimated Cycles regression to **−22.44%
  Ir / −36.17% Estimated Cycles** on `realloc_grow`, with the 15.80×→1.06×
  RSS win preserved (`CHANGELOG.md:516-524`). **Gate: GO, but recommendation
  only** — "`exact-span-large` + `large-reserved-capacity` remain opt-in,
  promotion left to the user" (`CHANGELOG.md:524`). Per Round 16's own
  changelog note (`CHANGELOG.md:303-306`), the user was asked via
  `AskUserQuestion` and confirmed **keep as-is** — i.e. this real win was
  explicitly NOT promoted into `production` even after being asked.
- `medium-classes` Stage-2 realloc promotion (R14-4, `3fde9f9`): iai shows
  +0.035% Ir (no regression) for a single-object growth chain, and alloc/free
  phases keep R10-2's original ~31×/~211× wins untouched
  (`docs/perf/R14_4_MEDIUM_REALLOC_PROMOTION_GATE.md:27-33`) — **but it does
  NOT clear R10-2's own realloc kill-gate** for R10-2's exact 16-object/
  8-slot-cache workload (still ~1,700–2,300× slower than baseline,
  `R14_4...md:388-399`), root-caused to cache-slot pressure, not a flaw in
  the promotion mechanism. Verdict: **CONDITIONAL-GO on the mechanism, RED on
  the kill-gate** (`R14_4...md §7`). Not promoted. `medium-classes` is not in
  `production`.
- `large-cache-extended`: CONDITIONAL-GO (R14-5), large real win on a
  turnover-shaped workload, but stays opt-in; its default budget was found
  oversized by external review and cut 1280→256 MiB/heap in R17-9
  (`1117198`, confirmed against `src/alloc_core/large_cache_config.rs:46-48,77-99`
  — `DEFAULT_HEADROOM_BYTES = 256 * 1024 * 1024`, `DEFAULT_EXTENDED_BUDGET_BYTES
  = DEFAULT_HEADROOM_BYTES`, i.e. the 5× multiplier is gone).

### 1c. Claimed but NOT statistically confirmed — the flagship example: `class-aware-dirty`

`class-aware-dirty` (R13-9, `da77b38`) is the **one** feature promoted into
`production` this era. Its original headline, "21.71× reduction," was a
sub-window timer inside the wallclock bench, not criterion's own full-round
mean (`docs/perf/R14_3_CLASS_AWARE_DIRTY_FIXED_WORK_AB.md:16-42` — R14-3
found the full round moved only ~11% at N=8 on the original measurement). The
project then ran the honest full-round, fixed-work, process-level A/B/B/A
protocol **four independent times** across two rounds:

| Measurement | mean(off) | mean(on) | paired t | significant? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| R14-3 run 1 (`R14_3...md:128-132`) | 57.38 ms | 62.45 ms | −2.148 | **significant — ON SLOWER** |
| R14-3 run 2, independent repeat | 99.49 ms | 122.01 ms | −1.404 | not significant |
| R17-7 warm-up run 1 (`R14_3...md §2.4`) | 53.39 ms | 54.62 ms | −0.714 | not significant |
| R17-7 warm-up run 2, independent repeat | 53.46 ms | 54.71 ms | −0.683 | not significant |

**Zero of four process-level measurements confirm a full-round production
speedup in the winning direction; one of the four crossed significance in
the *losing* direction.** Every same-vs-same harness-sanity control passed
(the harness itself is sound), so this is not a broken judge — it is a
genuine "we do not know" result. `class-aware-dirty` stays in `production`
"on **recoverability grounds** (it closes the R13-1 lost-wakeup class),
**NOT** on any confirmed speedup" (`CHANGELOG.md:136-139`,
`R14_3...md §2.4` final paragraph). This is the single clearest example in
the codebase of a *claimed* speedup that is honestly documented as
unconfirmed — and the project's own discipline (dual-axis reporting,
same-vs-same controls, environment-load disclosure) is exactly why this is
visible at all rather than papered over.

`R17-10`'s design doc (§0) independently confirms this reading: "R14-3 found
that class-aware-dirty's dramatic sub-window win ... does not survive at
nearly that magnitude on the full-round axis ... NOT statistically
significant in three of four process-level fixed-work re-measurements."

### 1d. Fixed regressions — "faster than before the regression" vs. "faster than ever"

This distinction matters and the codebase gives a clean example of each:

- **R17-3 is genuinely "faster than ever," not merely "regression
  recovered."** R16-4 had root-caused R15-1's flat Ir bump to R14-7's
  `MAX_SEGMENTS` 1024→4096 raise, measuring the raise's own increment at
  61,440 Ir. R17-3's fix removed the *entire* zero-fill loops, and the
  CHANGELOG is explicit that "these loops were never gated at ANY
  `MAX_SEGMENTS` value and predate R14-7" (`CHANGELOG.md:65-66`) — so the
  −81,966 Ir recovered is the pre-existing baseline cost (~20,480 Ir at
  `MAX_SEGMENTS=4096`, present since before Round 14) *plus* the raise's own
  61,440 Ir increment. Bootstrap cost today is lower than it was at any
  point in this project's history, not just lower than immediately before
  R14-7.
- **Contrast: R15-3's promotion-headroom fix (`4de4ef2`) is a pure
  regression-recovery, not a new win.** It compile-time-gates
  `try_promote_to_large` off in the zero-headroom triple-feature
  interaction, falling back to "the pre-existing, already-correct medium-
  ladder move leg, identical in shape to plain `production` without
  `medium-classes`" (`CHANGELOG.md:374-376`) — i.e. it restores the
  pre-R14-4 behavior for that configuration rather than improving on it.
- **R17-4's leak fix** (§1a above) is the same shape as R15-3: it removes a
  defect R14-4 introduced (the leak did not exist before Stage-2 promotion
  shipped), so the ~68 MiB post-fix commit figure is "as good as intended
  when R14-4 was designed," not a new ceiling below history.

### Overall verdict for Q1

Rounds 13–17, taken together, are overwhelmingly a **correctness and
soundness hardening arc** (unsafe-boundary closures R14-1/R14-2/R14-9/
R15-2/R15-4/R17-1/R17-2, a real segment leak found and fixed R17-4, CI
coverage gaps closed R13-5/R13-12/R16-1, doc-drift fixes R15-5/R16-2/R16-5/
R17-6), not a performance arc. The CHANGELOG's own "Production vs. opt-in"
paragraphs, present at the top of every round's entry since R13-10
(`CHANGELOG.md:214-227`, `:436-447`, `:626-640`), consistently confirm this:
each round states plainly that no feature-list change landed and each
production-touching fix is either soundness-only or iai-confirmed
zero-cost. The project's own honesty machinery (the R14-3 dual-axis rule,
the R17-7 re-verification, the CLAUDE.md wall-clock-gate rule it produced)
is itself evidence the team is not overclaiming — but it also means the
honest answer to "did we speed up `production`" for this specific five-round
window is: **not demonstrably, beyond one bootstrap-constant fix (R17-3) and
zero-cost soundness hardening.** The real, large, well-measured wins from
this era all live behind feature flags a default user does not get.

## 2. What else can be sped up meaningfully?

### 2.1 R17-10's own self-assessment — I agree with it

`docs/perf/R17_10_BATCHED_DEFERRED_RECLAIM_DESIGN.md` proposes two
sub-designs (batch the per-block decommit check; defer cross-segment
finalization within one drain sweep) and is explicit about its own ceiling:
"a constant-factor tightening, not an algorithmic class change, unlike
class-aware-dirty's own O(D)→O(D_class) win" (`R17_10...md:191-193`). Having
read the cost-accounting table (§1.4) and the two sub-designs (§2.1/§2.2)
closely: **this self-assessment is accurate, not falsely modest.**
Sub-design A saves "(N−1) avoided decrement+compare sequences" per segment
where each one is already O(1) — genuinely single-digit-nanosecond territory,
smaller than `class-aware-dirty`'s own win, which *itself* could not clear
statistical significance at 20-pair sample sizes (§1c above). Sub-design B's
entire value is conditional on an unmeasured empirical question (§5.1 stage
1: does a realistic workload ever empty >1 segment per sweep?) that the
document correctly refuses to guess at. I do not see a way to read this
design as understating its own potential — if anything the document is more
rigorous than most Round-13–17 perf docs about NOT overselling a change
before measuring it (the explicit two-stage "measure the counter first,
build the mechanism only if stage 1 justifies it" plan, §5.1, mirrors R9-6→
R12-7's already-successful discipline).

### 2.2 A bigger target the R17-10 doc does not cover — and says so explicitly

`R14_7_EXPANDABLE_SEGMENT_TABLE_DESIGN.md §4` (a Round-14 doc, not touched by
Round 17) contains a joint-evaluation table comparing the expandable
`SegmentTable` design against the page-run layer, and its "Cold-carve gap"
row states plainly: **"Does not help at all" / "Not evaluated for this
either" — "The cold-carve gap remains entirely unaddressed by either design
and would need its own investigation"** (`R14_7...md:349,359-363`). That gap
is `README.md:238`'s own current, live number: **"a 2.4–2.7× cold gap at
16 B/256 B and parity at 64 B"** versus `mimalloc` — still true as of the
README committed at the top of this review's window (I did not find a Round
13–17 commit that moved this number). The dedicated tracking document,
`docs/perf/PERF_PLAN_beat_mimalloc_small_medium.md`, lays out a concrete,
already-diagnosed, instruction-level root cause ("Sefer is flat ~28 µs
across all sizes ... instruction-bound, not page-fault-bound ... we pay for
*ceremony*") and five named "eurekas" (Э1–Э5) with a phased task chain
(#144–#149). Some of this chain visibly landed already (Э1's "bump-direct"
fast path appears as `regression_bump_direct_refill` in
`.github/workflows/ci.yml:567`), but the README's own current 2.4–2.7×
figure shows the front is **not closed**. This is a considerably larger,
already-root-caused opportunity than R17-10's constant-factor decommit
batching — it sits on the crate's own comparison front page (README
`## Measured gap`) as an acknowledged loss against a widely-used competitor,
not a hypothetical. I did not find any Round 13–17 task that revisited this
plan; it appears to have gone dormant since whenever Э1 landed. **Recommend
this as the next round's P1 candidate over further class-aware-dirty/
batched-reclaim tuning**, given class-aware-dirty's own full-round effect
still cannot be confirmed after four attempts (§1c) while the cold-carve gap
is large, reproducible, and has a committed root-cause diagnosis already
sitting unused.

### 3.4 `race_repro.rs` — a flake documented twice, root cause still "unconfirmed"

`tests/race_repro.rs:44-63` documents a **second** independent
`STATUS_STACK_BUFFER_OVERRUN` occurrence (Round 14, task #289; Round 17,
task #326) in `drain_reclaim_uaf_repro_tight_handoff`, both under heavy
concurrent CPU load in the shared dev workspace, both unreproduced across a
combined 100+ deliberate re-run attempts (3+20 in Round 14; 80 in Round 17
across three harsher load profiles). The file's own working hypothesis is
"a rare Windows scheduler/stack-guard artifact ... not a genuine allocator
defect" — explicitly labeled **unconfirmed**. I have **no independent
evidence either way** — I did not (and per this review's read-only
constraint, could not) attempt to reproduce it myself. This is worth flagging
precisely because the project's own methodology (zero-trust, red/green
counterfactuals everywhere else) has, for this one signature, settled for

`STATUS_STACK_BUFFER_OVERRUN` is a stronger signature than an ordinary
scheduling hiccup (it implies a stack-guard-page hit or a `/GS` cookie
check, not just a slow test) — if it recurs a third time, the investigation
should treat "confirmed environmental" as still an open hypothesis, not a
settled one, given two occurrences is a thin base for ruling out a genuine
rare corruption. **Not a confirmed defect — a documented suspicion the
project itself has not closed with certainty**, stated here per the review
brief's instruction not to present a suspicion as a fact.

## 4. What should improve in the project/process as a whole?

### 4.1 Is R17-4's "found late" pattern likely to recur elsewhere?

R17-4 is notable not just as a bug but as a **process** finding: the
anomaly it root-caused had been sitting as an explicitly-named open question
in `R14_4_MEDIUM_REALLOC_PROMOTION_GATE.md §2.2` since Round 14, restated as
still-open in the same doc's §6 through Round 16, and only closed in Round
17 because an external review's plan explicitly assigned "root-cause the
pad/cache admission anomaly" as its own P1 task (R17-4 in
`docs/reviews/2026-07-24-r17-plan.md:29`). Nothing about this bug's own
mechanics (a dealloc-dispatch keyed on `class_for(layout.size())` instead of
segment `kind`, only reachable when `medium-classes` combines with the
Stage-2 promotion's in-place OPT-G growth) would have been caught by any of
the CI jobs I read — it required a human (or reviewing agent) reading an
open question in a gate report and deciding to chase it, not a
structural/automated check.

**I looked for other candidates of the same shape** — an open question
recorded honestly in a gate doc, left unresolved across multiple subsequent
rounds — and found one: **§2.3 above (R14-4's own open item #2, the R10-2
gate re-run against R14-5's hardened cache)**, dropped for three rounds with
no re-run and no explicit re-deferral. I did not find others with the same
"explicitly named as open, then silently not chased" shape in the docs I
read, but I did not audit every `docs/perf/*.md §6/§Open items` section in
this codebase (there are dozens); **this is a "not fully audited, more likely
than not to have siblings" suspicion, not a claim that R14-4's item #2 is the
only one.** A lightweight process fix worth considering: a single running
tracking file (or a task-list convention) that lists every "open item flagged
in a gate report §6" across all `docs/perf/*.md`, checked at the start of
each new round's planning pass — the current mechanism (an external review
happening to re-read the right file) is exactly the ad-hoc mechanism that let
R17-4 sit for three rounds.

### 4.2 CI feature-combination coverage — verified, not a current blind spot

The task brief asked me to specifically check whether the
`cargo-hack --feature-powerset --depth 2` weekly job (adopted in R14-10
specifically because R13-12/#285's bug was invisible to every hand-written
CI row) actually covers the class of bug it was built for. I checked this
concern directly: R13-12's bug needed `alloc-xthread`+`fastbin`+
`alloc-decommit` simultaneously ON, without `alloc-segment-directory` — a
3-features-on combination, which a naive reading of "`--depth 2`" (limits
`cargo-hack`'s explicit selection to at most 2 features at a time) would
seem to miss. I verified this is **not actually a gap**: `Cargo.toml:385`
defines `fastbin = ["alloc-global", "alloc-xthread"]`, so `cargo-hack`
selecting just the 2-feature combination `{fastbin, alloc-decommit}` (an
in-depth-2 combination) resolves, via ordinary Cargo feature-dependency
propagation, to the full 4-feature set `{fastbin, alloc-decommit,
alloc-global, alloc-xthread}` — the exact configuration the original bug
needed, without `alloc-segment-directory` (not a dependency of any of
those). So the weekly job's own motivating claim is correct for this
specific case, because the crate's feature *dependency* graph (not the
explicit selection depth) supplies the extra arity. **This is a good design
property of the current Cargo.toml feature graph, not a coincidence to
assume holds for every future 3+-feature bug** — a hypothetical future bug
needing 3 features ON that do NOT chain through any single 2-feature
dependency edge would still be invisible to a depth-2 sweep. Worth noting
for whoever next tunes `--depth`, not an active gap today.

### 4.3 `perf-gate.yml` — a documented, deliberate non-blocking design, worth re-stating as a live tradeoff

`perf-gate.yml:1-25` runs the iai instruction-count regression gate only on
`schedule` (nightly), `workflow_dispatch`, or a PR explicitly labeled
`perf` — never on a normal push/PR by default. This is explicitly documented
as deliberate ("perf benchmarks ... are a bad fit for every push/PR"). Given
this project's actual workflow commits directly to `main` per task
(no visible PR-gating step in the commit history I read), a `production`-
path regression introduced mid-round would not be caught until the next
nightly run at the earliest — up to 24h of drift, by the job's own design.
This is a known, accepted, and clearly-commented tradeoff, not a hidden
blind spot — flagging it here only because the task brief asked specifically
about CI blind spots and this is the other (documented, not hidden) one
alongside the feature-powerset job.

### 4.4 Process observation: the project's own honesty machinery is its strongest asset, and Round 17 shows it still working

Worth stating plainly since three of the four review questions above are
critical: the dual-axis wall-clock rule (R14-3), the same-vs-same control
convention, the environment-load disclosure (R17-7), and the "recommend, the
orchestrator decides" promotion protocol are all *why* §1c's honest
non-finding is visible in this codebase at all, rather than a stale "21.71×"
headline still sitting in the README. Round 17 in particular re-ran a
measurement it could easily have skipped (R17-7, since `class-aware-dirty`'s
promotion decision was not being revisited) purely to close a P2 methodology
concern, and reported a null result honestly rather than declaring victory.
That discipline is the main reason this review was able to find concrete,
cited evidence for Q1's nuanced answer instead of having to guess.
